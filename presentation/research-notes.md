# SEE AMERICA / WHITE SANDS
## Editorial research and motion-design production notes — v2

**Status:** Research development. Not a finished poster, motion graphic, or final presentation.

### Editorial rules
Use specific, verifiable statements. Avoid travel-advertising filler, dramatic assertions unsupported by evidence, and fabricated quotations. The material should feel like an illustrated field journal, not promotional copy. Maintain exact location distinctions: White Sands National Park, White Sands Missile Range, Trinity Site, and Roswell are different places.

### Sequence 01 — The surface
**On screen:** WHITE SANDS / TULAROSA BASIN / NEW MEXICO

The dunes are gypsum, not the quartz sand familiar from many beaches. Wind continually moves the pale grains into ridges and slopes. The national park protects part of a much larger gypsum dunefield.

**Image needs:** One verified wide-angle NPS dune photograph, one close detail of ripples or grains, one map with park boundary distinguished from the larger basin.
**Motion:** Hold on a still photograph, then move through the image slowly. Keep the text stationary long enough to read.
**Source basis:** User-supplied White Sands research PDF, pp. 1–2; NPS White Sands Purpose and Significance.

### Sequence 02 — The animals that blend in
The dunes appear bare from a distance, but animals move through them. Some White Sands species have evolved unusually pale coloration. A bleached earless lizard against gypsum makes this adaptation visible without explanation-heavy copy.

**Image needs:** Species-identified wildlife image; remote-camera photographs of kit fox, badger, or skunk; native plant detail. Confirm each species, location, photographer, and license at the asset level.
**Motion:** Alternating macro and landscape scales; no artificial animal animation.
**Sources:** https://www.nps.gov/whsa/learn/management/white-sands-purpose-and-significance-statement.htm ; https://www.nps.gov/media/photo/gallery.htm?id=14D08678-155D-451F-6717B25DFB9C6CBA

### Sequence 03 — Before the dunes
Long before the present dunefield, Lake Otero occupied the basin. Human and animal footprints survived in sediments at the lake's edge. NPS research places some human tracks roughly 21,000–23,000 years in the past. The footprints are physical evidence of people moving through this landscape; their lives should not be invented for dramatic effect.

**Image needs:** Actual NPS fossil-track photographs and NPS public-domain footprint B-roll. Clearly label any scientific reconstruction as a reconstruction and check its separate copyright.
**Motion:** Photograph, geological strata annotation, slow transition to present-day landscape.
**Sources:** https://www.nps.gov/whsa/learn/nature/fossilized-footprints.htm ; https://www.nps.gov/whsa/learn/photosmultimedia/videos.htm

### Sequence 04 — The night and the range
The dunes reflect moonlight, while the broader Tularosa Basin has a documented military and aerospace history. The Trinity nuclear test occurred on July 16, 1945, roughly 60 miles north of the national park, at a site now within White Sands Missile Range. Military testing and park access are separate factual matters. UFO claims should only appear when attached to a traceable historical report, with the claim and evidence clearly distinguished.

**Image needs:** Verified night-sky photo with precise location; archival Trinity photograph from a rights-cleared government collection; map identifying park versus missile range.
**Motion:** Night-sky hold, archival cross-dissolve, map annotation. Do not fabricate flying objects, test footage, or archival-looking evidence.
**Source:** https://www.nps.gov/whsa/learn/news/parkstatistics.htm

### Sequence 05 — What a visitor finds
Walking, sledding, ranger programs, and watching the light change are documented visitor activities. Alamogordo is the practical nearby base for food and lodging. Restaurants and accommodations are image-research subjects, not endorsements; confirm current names, operations, and photo permissions before inclusion.

**Image needs:** NPS activities, actual Alamogordo restaurant exteriors and food images with permission, and accommodation photography with credited owners.
**Source basis:** User-supplied White Sands research PDF, pp. 3–4.

### Sequence 06 — Design study
Historical WPA and See America travel posters are research artifacts. Study the original date, creator, sponsor, print method, palette, and type hierarchy for each. Contemporary reinterpretations must be identified as such. Avoid recreating a specific poster's unique composition.

**Image needs:** Library of Congress archival record and full rights statement for each selected poster.

## Asset selection gate
- Exact place and subject confirmed.
- Original asset record retained (not a random repost or Pinterest derivative).
- Creator, date, source URL, and rights status recorded.
- Sufficient pixels for the intended animation crop; avoid excessive digital zoom.
- No AI-generated imagery presented as historical or documentary evidence.
- Reproduced charts or scientific reconstructions retain their own copyright attribution.

## Motion gate
- 1920 × 1080 / 30 fps working compositions.
- Reading holds determined by actual copy length; do not force a uniform scrolling speed.
- Transitions express geography, time, or scale rather than decorating every slide.
- No fake film damage, synthetic grain over archival records, unnecessary particles, or arbitrary zooms.
- Embed motion as video in PowerPoint; provide a static PDF companion. PDF is not the autoplay format.
